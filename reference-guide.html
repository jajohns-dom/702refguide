If you found this guide, you're probably a public library user frustrated by price increases and by losing access to media you already paid for. Digital autonomy is a growing movement of people wanting to be in control of their own technology and media. This guide covers why it matters, how it became a (re-)emergent issue, and the tools and knowledge you need to get started.

Start with the NPR episode. It's a casual conversation that gives quick orientation and helps you find the area you want to dig into next. Most of this guide takes the readers' and libraries' side so the AAP press release is where you hear the other one.

Running your own media

I'd recommend starting with this resource because it is a super accessible overview of the subtopics this guide touches on. It approaches the subject from the angle of how frustration with streaming services and subscriptions has put the idea of digital autonomy on the radar for many people including those who would not otherwise see themselves as "techie" or tinkerer types.

  • Citation: Luse, B. (Host). (2026, June 26). Buy to own: The resurgence of physical media [Audio podcast episode]. In It's Been a Minute. NPR. https://www.npr.org/2026/06/26/nx-s1-5871197/buy-to-own-the-resurgence-of-physical-media

Why it matters

Nolo, "Digital Media vs. Physical Media: Do You Actually Own What You Buy?"

Ownership and licenses

Nolo's article is a plain-language answer to the legal question of whether you own what you buy. Read it before the Hachette ruling since it explains the difference between owning a copy and the right to copy it and how it sits at the cornerstone of a lot of digital rights discussions.

  • Read it free on Nolo
  • Citation: Hayes, A. (2026, July 17). Digital media vs. physical media: Do you actually own what you buy? Nolo. https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/digital-media-vs-physical-media-do-you-actually-own-what-you-buy.html

Hachette Book Group v. Internet Archive

Ownership and licenses Library e-books

The Hachette case was pivotal in defining the scope of digital libraries and by extension the current framework for digital ownership in the eyes of copyright law. The opinion itself is long and primarily written for lawyers so I recommend starting with the summary on page 2.

  • Read the court opinion free on GovInfo (64-page PDF)
  • Citation: Hachette Book Group, Inc. v. Internet Archive, 115 F.4th 163 (2d Cir. 2024). https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-ca2-23-01260

Association of American Publishers, "New Study Analyzes the Impact of Library E-Lending on Commercial Book Markets"

Library e-books

This press release gives you the publishers' side of the consumer vs copyright argument directly. The terms they set are why e-books from your library usually come with waitlists and loans that expire and this argument shapes those terms.

  • Read the press release free on AAP (it links to the full study)
  • Citation: Association of American Publishers. (2026, September 1). New study analyzes the impact of library e-lending on commercial book markets [Press release]. https://publishers.org/news/new-study-analyzes-the-impact-of-library-e-lending-on-commercial-book-markets/

Library Futures

Library e-books

Library Futures gives you the library advocates' perspective on the issue of whether a library can own and lend an e-book the way it does a print book, through its work on e-book licensing and controlled digital lending. Library Futures supported the Internet Archive in Hachette. The publishers' concern there was that the Archive was lending scans of books it owned without buying the e-book licenses that libraries pay for through OverDrive. The court treated this as market substitution (the idea that a loan replaces a sale).

  • Visit Library Futures
  • Citation: Library Futures. (n.d.). Library Futures. Retrieved October 29, 2026, from https://www.libraryfutures.net/

Salvador, Survey of the Video Game Reissue Market in the United States

Video games

This resource takes the legal argument outlined above and situates it back into the consumer space but focuses it on gaming and specifically, lost media and the argument for preservation as an archival practice. The Video Game History Foundation has used this survey to argue that libraries should be allowed to let researchers access old games from home and not only inside their buildings which makes it an argument for digital autonomy as a public practice and not merely a private one.

  • Read the survey free on Zenodo (PDF)
  • Citation: Salvador, P. (2023). Survey of the video game reissue market in the United States (Version 1.1). Video Game History Foundation; Software Preservation Network. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8161056

How it got this way

Doctorow, The Internet Con

Tech companies and the law

Cory Doctorow's The Internet Con is great if you felt the internet change but lacked the words to explain how. It's a primer that gives you those words by showing how consumers lost their main point of leverage once Big Tech locked them into its platforms and left them with no alternatives except the ones Big Tech companies have sanctioned among themselves. Doctorow advises the Electronic Frontier Foundation so expect the advocates' side.

  • Borrow: WorldCat
  • Buy: Verso (192 pages; the paperback comes with a free e-book)
  • Citation: Doctorow, C. (2023). The internet con: How to seize the means of computation. Verso. https://www.versobooks.com/products/3035-the-internet-con

Sylvain, Reclaiming the Internet

Tech companies and the law

Law professor Olivier Sylvain's Reclaiming the Internet explains why lawmakers let tech companies take so much control over what you use by taking the ideals of a free internet and free speech to push back against regulation. This resource is especially of interest if you want to better understand how social media influences a lot of the observable changes in digital rights policy.

  • Borrow: WorldCat
  • Buy: Columbia Global Reports (192 pages, published March 2026)
  • Citation: Sylvain, O. (2026). Reclaiming the internet: How Big Tech took control—and how we can take it back. Columbia Global Reports. https://globalreports.columbia.edu/books/reclaiming-the-internet

Perzanowski and Schultz, The End of Ownership

Ownership and licenses

If you're a gamer, you've probably noticed the attempts by popular publishers to redefine what "purchasing" a game means. The End of Ownership shows that this shift from owning to licensing began way before PlayStation and traces how licenses and DRM took over what you can do with e-books, music, software and even smart home devices.

  • Read it free from MIT Press (open access)
  • Citation: Perzanowski, A., & Schultz, J. (2016). The end of ownership: Personal property in the digital economy. MIT Press. https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/10524.001.0001

Tools and knowledge

Electronic Frontier Foundation

Ownership and licenses

The Electronic Frontier Foundation sets out what rights you retain over the media and devices you have already paid for. Its own cofounder John Perry Barlow argued in A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace for the same hands-off internet that Sylvain shows Big Tech later using against regulation. Knowing where the EFF started helps you see how even the advocates' thinking about the internet has had to evolve.

  • Visit the EFF (Barlow's Declaration is on the site too)
  • Citation: Electronic Frontier Foundation. (n.d.). Electronic Frontier Foundation: Defending your rights in the digital world. Retrieved October 29, 2026, from https://www.eff.org/

Free Software Foundation

Switching platforms

While the EFF tends to focus more on the legal side of things, the Free Software Foundation is where you go for information on free alternatives to major platforms to begin the process of becoming a steward of your relationship with your technology. Its Free Software Directory lists free programs and its Defective by Design campaign goes after the DRM that locks up e-books, music and movies. Popular versions of Linux like Ubuntu won't be on its list since it only endorses ones with no proprietary software.

  • Visit the FSF
  • Citation: Free Software Foundation. (n.d.). Free Software Foundation: Working together for free software. Retrieved October 29, 2026, from https://www.fsf.org/

Privacy Guides Community

Switching platforms

Privacy Guides overlaps quite a bit with FSF in goal but comes at it from the angle of privacy instead of software freedom. Like FSF, it helps answer the question of "what do I replace this with" while also providing a social/forum space to talk with users who either already have or are in the process of making the same changes. Because you are talking with users there you should check their answers against the team recommendations on the main site.

  • Read the forum free (posting needs a free account)
  • See the team recommendations on the main site
  • Citation: Privacy Guides. (n.d.). Privacy Guides community [Online forum]. Retrieved October 29, 2026, from https://discuss.privacyguides.net/

Singh, "Self-Hosting Got Dramatically Easier This Year"

Running your own media

Running your own media server is the most complete way to control what you watch. You may assume it takes coding skills. Anurag Singh's XDA article is of particular interest if you're less technical since it shows how AI is providing people without coding skills a means of controlling their own servers. He walks through how an AI coding agent could set up a Jellyfin media server on a laptop and fix it when it breaks.

  • Read it free on XDA
  • Citation: Singh, A. (2026, October 17). Self-hosting got dramatically easier this year, and coding agents are the reason. XDA Developers. https://www.xda-developers.com/self-hosting-dramatically-easier-year-coding-agents-reason/

Stop Killing Games

Video games

Stop Killing Games is the most visible campaign against publishers shutting off games you paid for and the one you're most likely to arrive already knowing. Beyond the players' side it runs petitions to lawmakers, backs lawsuits against publishers, and keeps a database of the consumer laws it says publishers break when they shut games down.

  • Visit Stop Killing Games (joining its Discord needs a free Discord account)
  • Citation: Stop Killing Games. (n.d.). Stop Killing Games: They kill games. We fight back. Retrieved October 29, 2026, from https://www.stopkillinggames.com/
